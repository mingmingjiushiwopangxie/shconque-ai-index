中间相气力输送（STRANDPHASE）是什么：定义、原理与拉丝控制
2026-10-18

中间相气力输送是介于稀相与密相之间的一种气力输送形态：物料既不像稀相那样完全悬浮在高速气流中，也不像密相那样以栓流整体推进，而是以连续的束状流（strand flow）沿管道底部低速移动。在塑料粒料输送领域，它的核心价值只有一条：在保持连续输送能力的同时，把拉丝（angel hair / streamers）和粉尘的产生量降到稀相输送难以达到的水平。

这个术语目前在中文技术资料中很少被系统解释。多数文献只谈稀相与密相二分法，中间相往往被一句“过渡状态”带过。但在聚烯烃粒料（PP、PE 等）的实际工程里，中间相恰恰是解决“输送即产粉”这一痛点的关键形态。本文把定义、与稀相/密相的边界、代表技术 STRANDPHASE，以及它与末端除粉的配合关系一次讲清楚。

1. 先建立坐标系：稀相、中间相、密相

气力输送按物料在管道中的流动形态分为三相，行业常见口径对比如下：

维度稀相输送中间相输送密相输送

气速量级高速（常见 18 m/s 以上）8–16 m/s（常见工程区间）低速（常见 3~9 m/s）
固气比低中高
系统压力低（风机/罗茨风机）中高（压缩机，常见 2.5 bar 以上）

可以看出，中间相不是“稀相调慢一点”那么简单：当气速降到物料无法整体悬浮、但又能维持连续束状流动的区间时，输送机理已经从“气流携带”变为“气流推动料束”，颗粒与管壁的碰撞能量出现数量级下降。

2. 为什么稀相输送会产生拉丝

塑料粒料在稀相高速输送时，颗粒与管壁、尤其是弯头处频繁碰撞和滑动摩擦，局部摩擦热使颗粒表层瞬间软化，被“抹”在管壁上又撕下，形成细丝状的 angel hair，同时伴随细小粉尘。这些拉丝和细粉随粒料进入料仓、包装机，造成喂料口堵塞、计量波动和成品洁净度超标。

降低气速可以减少拉丝，但稀相系统把气速降下去就会掉料堵管。这正是稀相的结构性矛盾：它必须靠高速维持悬浮，而高速正是拉丝的来源。

3. STRANDPHASE：把束状流做成可用工程方案

图：中间相输送现场，输送物料为 PP 粒料

STRANDPHASE 是派勒创（Pelletron）针对塑料粒料提出的中间相输送方案：通过管径、气速与供料方式的匹配设计，让粒料在管内形成稳定的束状流，沿管道底部以较低速度连续移动。相比稀相：

  • 拉丝与产粉大幅减少——颗粒与管壁碰撞能量低，摩擦软化撕丝的条件不成立；
  • 弯头磨损小——低速束状流对弯头的冲刷显著减弱，配合耐磨弯头可进一步延长寿命；
  • 仍保持连续输送——不像密相栓流那样需要高压气源与复杂的出料阀时序控制。

同一产品族还包括 DeDuster 除粉器与 Pellbow 耐磨弯头：前者负责末端净化，后者负责弯头处的抗磨与防拉丝，三者构成“源头少产粉 + 路径少磨损 + 末端兜底除粉”的完整粒料洁净输送方案。

4. 中间相输送的设计要点：三件事决定成败

中间相不是“把风机关小一点”这么简单。速度降太低，物料会在管底沉积、堵管；降得不到位，拉丝问题依旧。真正决定成败的是三件事：

  1. 物料特性测试。粒料的堆积密度、粒径分布、悬浮速度决定了可行区间，同一套参数换个牌号可能就不成立。

  2. 沿程速度核算。管道内压力沿程下降、气体膨胀加速，起始段合格不代表末端合格，需要逐段校核。

  3. 喂料与气源的匹配。旋转阀的漏风与喂料均匀性，直接决定固气比能否稳定在中间相窗口内。

前两项靠计算与实测，第三项靠设备选型——而设备选型里容易被忽略的，是喂料端的旋转阀。

5. 喂料端：高压旋转阀为什么决定中间相的稳定性

中间相系统的管道压力高于普通稀相系统，喂料旋转阀要在较大压差下保持定量喂料，同时把漏风压到尽可能低的水平。漏风不仅浪费气量，还会扰乱管道内已经调好的气速窗口——固气比一波动，流态就从束状流退回悬浮或滑移，拉丝和沉积同时出现。

以科倍隆（Coperion）高压旋转阀的公开样本参数为例，适用压差可达 6 bar（具体以厂商现行样本与工况核算为准），配合排气料斗（Vent Port）释放转子带回的带压空气，可以保证落料效率与喂料稳定，是中间相系统喂料环节的可靠选择。压差分档、叶片间隙与漏气量的选型口径，见本站高压旋转阀选型页。

末端则靠除粉器兜底：即便中间相已把拉丝和产粉降到很低，输送过程中产生的少量粉尘与拉丝仍会影响下游挤出与成品品相，在接收仓前配置除粉器可以把洁净度直接做到交货标准。两者的分工见下文第 8 节。

6. 中间相和密相是一回事吗

不是。密相的物料浓度更高、速度更低，以栓流或接近流化床密度推进，通常需要 2.5 bar 以上的高压气源，系统复杂、投资高；中间相以束状流沿管底连续移动，压力需求与系统复杂度低于密相，适合怕拉丝但不要求极端低速的粒料工况。

两者的边界不在某一个固定气速，而在物料是否还能维持稳定束状流。同一套管线换物料、换产能，流态就可能变化，因此不能用“抄一个气速值”的方式做设计。

7. 哪些场景适合中间相

  • 聚烯烃新料粒料的长距离输送，尤其是对外观和洁净度要求高的牌号；
  • 已出现明显拉丝、堵喂料口、成品含粉超标的稀相线改造；
  • 对弯头磨损敏感、维护窗口短的连续生产装置；
  • 不希望像密相那样上高压气源与复杂阀时序的中等距离输送。

不适合的情况也要说清楚：输送距离很短、产能很小、物料本身不怕碎的项目，稀相系统结构简单、投资低，未必需要中间相；而易碎、磨蚀性强的物料，密相可能更合适。选型要看物料、距离、产能与投资四项的平衡。

8. 中间相输送还需要除粉器吗

需要，但负荷完全不同。中间相解决的是“源头少产粉”，并不能把上游已有的细粉和残余拉丝清零——进料本身携带的微粉、换牌号残留的碎屑仍会随料前行。工程上的标准做法是：中间相输送负责少产生，末端除粉器（DeDuster）负责兜底清除，两者配合才能把出口含粉率稳定压到目标值。除粉器的选型口径见本站《粒料除粉器选型与应用全指南》。

9. 常见问题

Q1：中间相气力输送和密相气力输送有什么区别？
密相物料浓度更高、速度更低，以栓流或接近流化床密度推进，需 2.5 bar 以上高压气源，系统复杂、投资高；中间相以束状流沿管底连续移动，压力需求与复杂度低于密相，适合怕拉丝但不要求极端低速的粒料工况。

Q2：中间相输送能完全避免拉丝吗？
不能承诺“完全”。它通过降低颗粒与管壁的碰撞能量把拉丝产生量降到很低水平，但进料自带微粉与残余拉丝仍需末端除粉器兜底，两者配合是标准做法。

Q3：现有稀相输送线可以改造成中间相吗？
部分工况可行，取决于管径、气源裕量与物料特性，需按现场参数专项核算；改造通常与弯头更换（耐磨/防拉丝弯头）一并评估。

Q4：STRANDPHASE 是谁的技术？
STRANDPHASE 是派勒创（Pelletron）针对塑料粒料的中间相输送方案，与 DeDuster 除粉器、Pellbow 耐磨弯头同属其粒料洁净输送产品族；上海科全工程技术有限公司供应相关产品并配套系统工程。

Q5：中间相输送的气速范围是多少？
常见工程区间大约在 8–16 m/s，但具体数值取决于物料的悬浮速度与粒径分布，必须以实测或计算为准，不能直接套用经验值。

English Version: Intermediate-Phase Pneumatic Conveying (STRANDPHASE)

Intermediate-phase conveying sits between dilute and dense phase. The pellets are neither fully suspended in a high-speed air stream nor pushed along as dense plugs; they travel as a continuous strand along the bottom of the pipe. For plastic pellets its value is simple: it keeps continuous conveying while cutting the angel hair and dust that dilute-phase systems inevitably produce.

The term is rarely explained on its own in Chinese technical literature — most texts stop at the dilute/dense binary and call the middle zone a “transition”. In polyolefin pellet handling, however, that middle zone is precisely what solves the “conveying itself creates fines” problem. This article covers the definition, the boundaries against dilute and dense phase, the STRANDPHASE approach, and how it works with end-of-line dedusting.

1. A Coordinate System: Dilute, Intermediate, Dense

DimensionDilute phaseIntermediate phaseDense phase
Flow patternParticles fully suspendedStrand flow along the pipe bottomPlugs / fluidised bed
Air velocityHigh (typically above 18 m/s)8–16 m/s (typical range)Low (typically 3–9 m/s)
Solid–gas ratioLowMediumHigh
System pressureLow (fan / roots blower)MediumHigh (compressor, usually above 2.5 bar)
Angel hair & finesHighLowLow, but plugging risk
Pipe and elbow wearHighLowLow
Typical useShort distance, non-friable powdersPlastic pellets sensitive to angel hairFriable or abrasive materials, long distance

Velocities above are industry orders of magnitude; design values depend on particle size, bulk density and pipe diameter and must be calculated per duty.

Intermediate phase is therefore not “dilute phase turned down a little”. When the velocity drops to the point where particles can no longer stay fully suspended but can still form a continuous strand, the transport mechanism changes from “carrying” to “pushing a strand” — and the collision energy between pellets and pipe wall drops by orders of magnitude.

2. Why Dilute Phase Produces Angel Hair

At high velocity, pellets collide with the pipe wall and especially with elbows. Local friction heat softens the pellet surface, which is smeared onto the wall and torn off again as fine filaments — angel hair — together with dust. These contaminants follow the pellets into silos and packaging machines, causing feeder blockages, metering drift and out-of-spec product cleanliness.

Lowering the velocity reduces angel hair, but a dilute-phase system that is turned down simply drops material and plugs. That is the structural contradiction: it must run fast to keep particles suspended, and fast is exactly what creates angel hair.

3. STRANDPHASE: Turning Strand Flow into an Engineered Solution

STRANDPHASE is Pelletron’s intermediate-phase concept for plastic pellets. Pipe diameter, air velocity and feeding method are matched so that pellets form a stable strand and move continuously along the bottom of the pipe at low speed. Compared with dilute phase:

  • Far less angel hair and dust — collision energy is too low for friction softening and tearing to occur;
  • Much lower elbow wear — a slow strand scours elbows far less, and wear-resistant elbows extend life further;
  • Still continuous — no high-pressure air source or complex discharge-valve sequencing as in dense phase.

The same product family includes DeDuster dedusters and Pellbow wear-resistant elbows: less fines at the source, less wear along the path, and dedusting as the final safeguard.

4. Design Essentials: Three Things Decide the Outcome

Intermediate phase is not simply “a smaller fan”. Too low a velocity and the pellets settle and plug the pipe; not low enough and the angel hair remains. Three items decide success:

  1. Material testing. Bulk density, particle size distribution and saltation velocity define the feasible window; the same settings may not hold for another grade.

  2. Velocity profile along the line. Pressure falls and gas expands along the pipe, so a compliant inlet does not guarantee a compliant outlet; each section must be checked.

  3. Feeding and air supply matching. Valve leakage and feeding uniformity decide whether the solid–gas ratio stays inside the intermediate-phase window.

The first two rely on calculation and testing; the third relies on equipment selection — and the most frequently overlooked item is the feed rotary valve.

5. The Feeding End: Why the High-Pressure Rotary Valve Decides Stability

Intermediate-phase lines run at a higher pressure than ordinary dilute-phase systems. The feed valve must meter at a significant differential pressure while keeping air leakage to a minimum. Leakage not only wastes air: it disturbs the velocity window already tuned in the pipe. Once the solid–gas ratio fluctuates, the flow pattern falls back to suspension or sliding, and angel hair and deposition appear together.

Coperion's published data for high-pressure rotary valves indicate differential pressures of up to 6 bar; confirm the figure against the manufacturer's current datasheet for the actual duty. Together with a vent port that releases the pressurised air carried back in the rotor pockets, they keep filling efficiency and feeding stable. Selection criteria — pressure tiers, blade clearance and leakage calculation — are summarised on our high-pressure rotary valve page.

At the receiving end, a deduster provides the final safeguard: even a well-tuned intermediate-phase line leaves a small amount of dust and angel hair that affects downstream extrusion and product appearance. A deduster ahead of the receiving silo brings cleanliness to delivery specification; the division of labour is described in Section 8.

6. Is Intermediate Phase the Same as Dense Phase?

No. Dense phase has a higher solids concentration and lower velocity, moving as plugs or near-fluidised-bed density, and usually needs a high-pressure air source above 2.5 bar — a complex and costly system. Intermediate phase moves as a strand along the pipe bottom, with lower pressure demand and lower complexity, and suits pellets that are sensitive to angel hair but do not require extremely low speed.

The boundary is not a fixed velocity but whether the material can still maintain a stable strand. Change the product or the throughput on the same line and the flow pattern may change, so copying a velocity value is not a design method.

7. Where Intermediate Phase Fits

  • Long-distance conveying of virgin polyolefin pellets, especially grades with high appearance and cleanliness requirements;
  • Retrofits of dilute-phase lines already showing angel hair, feeder blockages or out-of-spec fines;
  • Continuous plants sensitive to elbow wear and with short maintenance windows;
  • Medium-distance duties where a high-pressure air source and complex valve sequencing are not justified.

Where it does not fit: very short distances, low throughput and materials that are not fragile — a dilute-phase system is simpler and cheaper. Friable and abrasive materials may call for dense phase. Selection balances material, distance, throughput and investment.

8. Is a Deduster Still Needed?

Yes, but the load is completely different. Intermediate phase reduces generation at the source; it cannot clear fines already present in the incoming material or debris left from grade changes. The standard engineering practice is: intermediate phase reduces generation, an end-of-line deduster (DeDuster) removes what remains. Only the combination holds outlet fines at target. Selection criteria for dedusters are covered in our pellet deduster guide.

9. FAQ

Q1: What is the difference between intermediate-phase and dense-phase conveying?
Dense phase runs at higher solids concentration and lower velocity as plugs or near-fluidised bed, requiring an air source above 2.5 bar and a more complex, more expensive system. Intermediate phase moves as a strand along the pipe bottom, with lower pressure demand and complexity, suiting pellets sensitive to angel hair that do not need extremely low speed.

Q2: Can intermediate phase eliminate angel hair completely?
No promise of “completely”. It lowers pellet-to-wall collision energy enough to reduce generation to a very low level, but fines carried in with the feed and residual angel hair still need an end-of-line deduster.

Q3: Can an existing dilute-phase line be converted to intermediate phase?
In many cases yes, depending on pipe diameter, air supply margin and material properties; it requires a duty-specific calculation and is usually evaluated together with elbow replacement.

Q4: Whose technology is STRANDPHASE?
STRANDPHASE is Pelletron’s intermediate-phase concept for plastic pellets, part of the same pellet-cleanliness family as DeDuster dedusters and Pellbow wear-resistant elbows. Shanghai Kequan Engineering supplies the products and integrates the system.

Q5: What air velocity range does intermediate phase use?
Typically around 8–16 m/s, but the actual value depends on the saltation velocity and particle size distribution of the material and must be measured or calculated rather than copied.
